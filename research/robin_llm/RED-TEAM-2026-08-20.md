# Red team of the first bench lists — and what it changed

**Date:** 2026-08-20. Fifteen milled ideas were triaged by hand, then the triage itself was
red-teamed with live web search. The red team killed half of my own recommendations. Everything
below is what was checked and what it cost.

---

## What the hand triage claimed

Four of fifteen ideas were called "worth starting". Two of those calls rested on the same
inference: **the local arXiv corpus retrieved nothing close, therefore the idea is novel.**

## What the web search found

| claim | check | result |
|---|---|---|
| A8 "verification heads on a frozen base" is the most novel of the batch | one search | **Refuted.** UHeads (ICLR 2026 submission) trains uncertainty heads on the internal states of a *frozen* LLM to verify reasoning steps. Also CLUE (hidden-state clustering), one-token verification |
| B5 "attention-head sparsity" is genuinely unmeasured | one search | **Refuted.** [arXiv:2505.20045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20045), "Efficient Hallucination Detection Using Uncertainty-Aware Attention Heads" — a small subset of heads is uncertainty-aware and attention drops on hallucinated tokens |
| the white-box vs black-box wedge is an open question | one search | **Overstated.** Published head-to-heads exist ([2606.06959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06959): white-box MIND 75.45 vs black-box family average 66.07 on the same model). Our de-glossed rerun is still worth doing — our own result is currently INVALID — but it settles *our* number, not the field's |
| A1 restates a real paper | fetched | **Confirmed real.** [2607.09693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09693) "Depth-Entropy Guided Sampling for Training-Free LLM Reasoning" |
| A4 restates a real paper | fetched | **Confirmed real.** [2608.04719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04719) "Diagnosing Tool-Selection Reasoning in LLM Agents with Canary Tools" |

Two of two novelty claims died on the first query. The corpus is real — the future-dated ids
resolve to actual papers — but **BM25 over 124k abstracts is not a prior-art search**, and I used
it as one.

## The three failure modes, named

1. **Silence read as novelty.** The corpus not returning something is a fact about the corpus and
   the query, not about the world. This is the one that produced both wrong calls.
2. **The lab notebook is invisible to everyone.** Four of fifteen ideas were already answered
   *inside this repository* — a probe ladder at 0.837/0.980/1.000 by scale, `canary_hnorm` at
   AUROC 0.519, `humaneval_rerank.py` doing best-of-N with self-consistency, the verified cascade
   shipping. No web search will ever surface any of it.
3. **Retrieval-grounded generation produces retrieval-shaped ideas.** Three of eight ideas on one
   list were the title of a paper the generator had been shown. The grounding that makes an idea
   credible is the same thing that makes it unoriginal, and the sham control cannot catch it — a
   restated real paper *is* a good idea, just not a new one.

## What was built in response

[`priorwork.js`](priorwork.js) indexes 432 experiment scripts, research notes, ADRs and result
files by their own first paragraph, so failure mode 2 is automatic. [`novelty.js`](novelty.js)
audits each idea against three sources and returns one of six verdicts — **none of which is
"novel"**, which is failure mode 1 made unrepresentable. `UNVERIFIED` is the floor, and it carries
the web queries that would have to come back empty before anyone says the word.

The auditor is itself audited, every run: a paper from the shown set is replanted as an idea (must
come back `RESTATES`) and an idea this repo demonstrably answered is planted (must come back
`ANSWERED-HERE`). If either slips through, every verdict in the run is marked untrusted. Both
plants failed on the first attempt — the restatement plant was passing only a title, so the
auditor was judging a citation rather than a proposal, and said `UNVERIFIED` correctly. That is
the control firing on a construction bug, which is what controls are for.

**What it cannot do:** the web leg is not wired in. The repo's own search client returns confident
nonsense — asked about attention heads and hallucination detection it returned a Wikipedia article
on Lewy body dementia, with `success: true` — and a prior-art check that silently returns garbage
is worse than none. So the audit emits the queries and refuses to certify.

## Verified against the red team

Re-run on the four ideas judged by hand, the audit agrees where it can and stays silent where it
cannot:

| | hand triage | audit |
|---|---|---|
| B1 probe on activations | answered here | `ANSWERED-HERE` → `experiments/sigma0_hneurons_probe.py` |
| B4 paraphrase consistency | restated / weak | `INCREMENTAL` → cites `2607.11414` |
| B5 attention-head sparsity | *called novel — wrong* | `UNVERIFIED` + the queries that find 2505.20045 |
| B6 cross-layer activation norm | refuted here | `UNVERIFIED` — recall miss, but it does not claim novelty |

B6 is the honest limit: the refutation lives in `ouro_canary_vs_logprob.py` and its phrasing
("hidden-state norm") does not lexically reach the idea's ("activation norm ratio"). BM25 misses
it. The system reports `UNVERIFIED` rather than inventing a verdict — which is the correct
behaviour under imperfect recall, and the reason the vocabulary has no "novel" in it.
