How the decision is made

Measured evidence, not vibes

Support and resistance zones with touch counts, RSI, MACD, candle pattern, one-minute market structure, volume ratio and ATR — all computed in-process from live market bars.

A number, not an opinion

That evidence goes through a linear model that outputs a win probability and an expected value in R, then a decision: enter, or skip. The arithmetic is fixed and the same for every symbol.

The declines are published too

A setup can score well and still be refused — a trend-alignment filter turns down candidates that are not with the regime. Those refusals carry the rule that fired, and they sit on this page next to the accepted ones.

Ask it about any of this

The assistant is the part of Unisona that genuinely is AI — a language model wired to the same market tools the engine uses. Ask it what the current read on a symbol is and it calls the tool, shows you the call, and answers from the result rather than from memory. Every answer names the model that produced it.

The assistant reads the engine and explains it. It does not score the setups and it does not place trades — the two are deliberately separate, so a model having a bad day cannot move a number the engine computed.

Guide

What this is and how to use it, start to finish.

Unisona publishes the output of a deterministic scoring engine and a research assistant. Nothing here is investment advice, a recommendation, or an offer, and no figure on this page is a performance claim. Direction reads and win probabilities are the engine's classification of current market structure, not a forecast, and they change as the market does.
