Trader Guide

From zero to a running autopilot: create an account, connect your own Alpaca paper account, choose what runs, turn it on — and turn it off. The autopilot is off by default and only ever trades your account after you switch it on yourself.

1 Create your account

  • Open Sign in → Create account, enter your name, email and a password, and accept the terms.
  • Check your email for a 6-digit code (sender no-reply@unisona.ai) and type it into the boxes — you can't log in until the address is verified.
  • Log in with your email and password.

No code? Check spam. Stay on the page — the code entry doesn't navigate away.

2 Connect your Alpaca paper account

The trader runs on your own Alpaca account. We never see your Alpaca password; pasted keys are encrypted on the server and never shown, logged or shared.

  • Create a free account at alpaca.markets.
  • In Alpaca: Paper Trading → API Keys → Generate.
  • Here: Settings → Connections → Alpaca → Connect, paste the API Key ID (PK…) and Secret Key.
The server validates the keys against Alpaca before storing them — a typo fails immediately; nothing broken is ever saved. Paper keys only: the form refuses live keys by design.

3 Choose whose money, and which strategy

Account mode — whose money the trader acts on:

DemoRead-only tour of the Champion book. Can never place an order — enforced on the server.
PaperYour own practice account: your connected Alpaca paper account (or a house practice ledger until you connect one).
TradeLive money. Needs live keys and separate arming — not available through the normal flow.

Trader — Settings → Connections → “Autopilot on your account”:

⏸ OffNothing runs. The autopilot never touches your account. This is the default.
📈 IntradayThe day-trader: automated washout entries and ladder exits on liquid ETFs — your own manual buy/sell works alongside.
🏆 ChampionThe slow allocation book: a diversified ETF portfolio rebalanced on a schedule. Pauses the intraday trader.

One account, one active strategy. Switching takes effect on the next scan (~1 minute).

4 Turn it on — what the autopilot actually does

Flip the Trader switch from Off to Intraday (or Champion). That's the whole arming step. The intraday autopilot then:

  • Scans about once a minute during market hours.
  • Buys only (no shorting), on liquid ETFs from a curated watchlist, when a symbol is washed out — trading at the bottom of its session range.
  • Sizes positions as a percentage of your equity, caps how many are open at once, and always keeps a cash reserve.
  • Places a protective stop at the broker the moment a position opens — the stop exists even if the app goes down.
  • Exits on a ladder: banks profit at the first resistance, lets a runner ride through the second, then trails it. Losses exit at the stop.
  • Journals everything — every entry, every exit with P&L, and every opportunity it declined, with the reason.
Plan note: paper trading needs the Pro plan; the autonomous AI trader is a Pilot capability. Pricing shows what each plan includes.

5 Turn it off

  • Pause the autopilot: Trader switch → ⏸ Off. Takes effect within ~1 minute. Off is fully hands-off: it won't open or close anything — open positions are yours to manage, and their broker-side stops stay in place.
  • Disconnect Alpaca: Settings → Connections → Alpaca → Disconnect. Your keys are deleted from our server; positions and stops remain in your Alpaca account (it was always your account — we never had custody).
  • Delete your account: Settings → Account. Broker credentials are removed with it.

6 When something seems off

Connect failsRegenerate the key pair in Alpaca (the Paper section!) and paste both values fresh.
"It isn't trading"Is the Trader switch on? Is the market open? The journal's skip log lists every declined opportunity with its reason — most "not trading" is the trader correctly declining.
A position closed "by itself"Check the journal: stops and ladder exits are recorded with their reason.
"Not signed in" flickersThe server may be restarting — reload in ~30 seconds.
