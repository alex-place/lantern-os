The plans, and what each one does

Free covers guests and signed-in users; you pay to go further.

Guest and signed-in use are one free experience — a guest looks around read-only, and signing in saves your work. You pay when you want to trade for real with your own accounts, and again for an AI advisor that studies the market with you.

Watch a live demo account trade in real time (read-only, no sign-up), then create an account to get your own Alpaca paper-trading account, charts you personalize, and a chat that remembers you.

Pro

~$20 / mo

The real thing, your way

Everything in Free, plus connect your own brokerage to trade real money and plug in your own AI keys — with price alerts and an automatic journal that logs and tags every trade so you improve.

Business

$200 / mo

An AI advisor in your corner

Everything in Pro, plus UnisonaTrader — an AI stock-and-options advisor that researches the market with you and shows its evidence — and backer status: a say in what we build next.

03

Why people pick it

What makes someone choose us — in their words.

vs. ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini

They're brilliant, but they forget you and live in someone else's cloud. unisona is yours: your memory stays on your machine, any AI can power it, and it can actually do things — trade, handle files, hunt for jobs — the big providers won't touch.

vs. Robinhood + a chatbot

Robinhood makes trading a game and never says when it's unsure. Our desk shows live prices, flags stale or shaky data, rates how confident a signal is, and keeps a journal that makes you better.

vs. Perplexity / research tools

They cite sources for answers. We carry the evidence all the way through actions — a trade idea comes with its receipts, and the app flags anything it's only guessing at.

vs. build-it-yourself open source

Same "you own it" spirit, but it's a finished product — broker connection, document tools, job search, creator tools — not a kit you assemble yourself.

05

Getting the first 50 people

Prove it works before inviting anyone in.

Not an ad campaign — a hands-on program. And it starts by making sure nothing is broken.

  1. First: clear the roadblocks and prove the whole thing works. Before a single real person is invited, fix the known blockers (the broken "bring your own keys," the guest-experience papercuts) and run the entire journey end-to-end for 10 demo accounts: sign up → open a paper account → set up a watchlist → place a paper trade → chat about it → hit an upgrade prompt → (on Pro) connect a test broker and your own keys → see the journal log the trade. Every step has to pass green on all 10 accounts. We don't invite people onto a broken path.
  2. Open the doors. Anyone can sign up without a paywall; every new account starts with a practice trading account and a demo watchlist.
  3. Ten founding members. Hand-picked from the trader and creator circles we already know. Each gets a 30-minute welcome call, a founding-member badge, and three free months for weekly feedback.
  4. One community at a time. Post the live-trading clip in one paper-trading Discord and one relevant subreddit — "connect your own brokerage, we never touch your money." No spamming five channels.
  5. A four-week paper-trading contest. Practice accounts, a live leaderboard, small prizes. Everyone who plays ends up using both the trading desk and the chat.
  6. Count only what's real. "Active" means: set up a watchlist, had ten real chats, made one practice trade. Measured honestly, reviewed every week.
06

The hard questions, answered straight

What a sharp investor asks — and the honest reply.

“How many real users do you have?”

Two paying supporters, and honestly zero we've measured properly yet. Measuring it is the first month's work. An investor who sees us refuse to count fake usage trusts every other number we show.

“Won't OpenAI or Google just build this?”

They'll copy pieces. They won't let you own your data, won't route across their competitors, and won't connect to your brokerage — the first two hurt their business, the third is a risk they won't take.

“Trading plus AI sounds like a legal minefield.”

People trade their own accounts with their own login; we never hold anyone's money. The app shows evidence, it doesn't shout “buy.” We're getting a lawyer's review before we market real-money trading.

“What stops this from being just a wrapper?”

Three things that grow over time: a memory of you that's painful to leave behind, a trust layer that shows its work, and an app you install and own rather than a browser tab.

“Just one founder?”

A small human team plus an unusually productive set of AI assistants that ship work every day. The process itself is written down, so it doesn't live in one person's head.

“What could kill it?”

A trading-rules misstep (so we get the legal review first), spreading too thin (so we cut the weakest feature each quarter), or AI costs running away (so we push more of it local).

A heads-up on feedback we received. One review described “Humanize Intelligence,” a University of Kansas partnership, and “expert AI companions.” That's a different company with a similar name — none of it is us, and anything built on that description should be ignored.

Getting the word out

The app can help make its own marketing.

Three cheap, on-brand channels — all of which double as live demos.

  • A podcast, one episode per theme: the paper-trading contest recap; why your AI should remember you; teaching an AI to say “I don't know”; the Three Doors game; a weekly news roundup the app writes on camera; a “watch us build” episode; why charting apps lie; a creator-tools episode; a real user's first session, unedited; and the origin story — two people in a garage, and we lean into that.
  • A 24/7 stream the app generates itself — market open and close summaries, AI news read-outs, lo-fi in between — running as an always-on demo that feeds short clips to TikTok and Shorts.
  • Merch — a plushie of the lantern-bug mascot, signed prints of the hand-drawn game art, stickers, a “party on” tee, tied to podcast episodes and contest prizes. Marketing, not a money-maker.

Appendix — how this plan was edited

The original growth plan, kept / changed / cut.

The handed-over plan had the right shape — start small, welcome each early user by hand, grow by word of mouth. But it was written for a generic AI company, not this one. Here's the honest edit.

The ideaCallWhy
Grow step by step, welcome each early user personally, write down what workskeepRight for where we are — every welcome call doubles as customer research.
Post useful content a few times a weekkeepEasy for us: the app can help make its own content.
Chase real-estate agents, law firms, accountants, universitieschangeToo scattered. The people already paying us are a trader and a creator. Aim there first.
A $1,000-a-month planchangeNothing today justifies that price. The real ladder is Free / $20 Pro / $200 Business.
1,000 paying members and $100,000 a month within a yeardropThat needs five times our price and a sales team we don't have. Realistic is 120–300 members.
Raise investor money at the endchangeRight idea, wrong trigger — only once the app earns a real income and people demonstrably stick around.
The one thing that matters most: we can't honestly count our own users yet. Two people pay us and they're real, but we haven't measured how they use the app. Until we do, every growth number is a guess — which is why measuring real usage is Level 1's first job.
