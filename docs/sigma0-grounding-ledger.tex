\begin{abstract}
We compose three measured results about external grounding in self-improving loops into one
accounting frame. \textbf{Price}: a scoring signal's selection value decomposes by its error
structure --- a \emph{stuck} (checkpoint-fixed) error component sets a selection ratchet, a
\emph{fresh} (re-drawn) component breaks it, and only re-drawn external truth also ranks;
a pre-registered falsification of our own earlier law found that even zero-information
re-drawn noise (dither) recovers a large fraction of a stale holdout's lost value.
\textbf{Schedule}: inside a certified basin the anchor budget needed to escape grows
geometrically at the certified contraction rate and saturates, giving a computable grounding
cadence; critical-slowing-down alarms are structurally late (measured alarm premium
$2.25\times$), and which regime applies is decided --- prospectively --- by $\cond(P)$.
\textbf{Budget}: safe promotion rate is capped by fresh-task sourcing, stretched but never
uncapped by Thresholdout-style reuse; the mechanism buys scoreboard validity while an
accumulated burned pool buys extraction. Everything here is simulation- or
small-real-system-grade evidence; the markdown ledger is the source of record for evidence
classes and run pointers, and this document must never be cited above it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Price: what a scoring signal is worth}

Selection error decomposes as $\text{score} = \theta + e_{\text{stuck}} + e_{\text{fresh}}$.
The stuck part (a fixed holdout's lucky draw; a deterministic self-check's bias) anchors the
champion's seat; the fresh part re-rolls per gate and shakes it. Measured (32 seeds,
oracle-weight sweep per cell; kill test pre-registered and \emph{fired}):
zero-information dither reproduces the entire rescue of a stuck-bias signal
($6.42$ vs $6.43$ at $n{=}50$), while fresh truth strictly dominates every internal arm
($12.90 > 9.36 > 6.43 > 0.81 > 0.61$). The law, re-stated:
\emph{internal signals detect; fresh randomness de-ratchets; only fresh truth informs.}

\section{Schedule: when grounding must arrive}

Inside a certified basin $\{V \le c\}$ with $V(F(x)) \le \gamma V(x)$, $V = x^\top P x$:
\[
B^*(n) \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{c} - \gamma^{n/2}\sqrt{V_0}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(P)}}
\;\xrightarrow{\;n\to\infty\;}\; \sqrt{c/\lambda_{\max}(P)},
\qquad
t_{1/2} \;=\; \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1/\gamma)} .
\]
Ground on a cadence below $t_{1/2}$; the race (200 seeds, canary swept over all
healthy-quantile thresholds) measures an alarm premium of $2.25\times$ in the
well-conditioned regime and full timing-indifference in the sliver regime. The regime is
decided by $\cond(P)$, prospectively: on the trained \S6 reservoir (a real learned loop,
$\rho(J){=}0.9836$, $\cond(P){\approx}4.8{\cdot}10^4$) the pre-registered sliver call held
--- the realistic per-turn grounding kick is $1.4{\cdot}10^4\times$ the escape ceiling.
Both real learned loops measured so far (Ouro: expansive; reservoir: near-critical) sit
outside the well-conditioned regime; the schedule's bite may exist only on deliberately
JSRR-stabilized loops.

\section{Budget: how fast the system may safely improve}

$\text{rate(safe promotions)} \le \text{rate(fresh verified sourcing)}$, stretched by
controlled reuse. The Thresholdout decomposition (measured, then re-validated at task-level
Bernoulli-stuck fidelity): the \emph{mechanism} buys validity (reported-vs-true gap
$0.482 \to 0.187$ at $n{=}50$), the \emph{burned pool} buys extraction; retired promotion
sets remain useful. At task level the dither knob is the cheapest intervention
($0.617 \to 0.856$ true extraction at identical task cost). The planted teeth pass through
the real seven-condition gate with arm-appropriate reasons: naive reuse catches a
contaminated hack only via a provenance ledger; a fresh flow rejects it on measured merit.

\section{The composed operating point}

A self-improving loop must ground \emph{at least} on the schedule's cadence, may promote
\emph{at most} at the budget's rate, and must price signals by their stuck/fresh error
decomposition. All three constraints are computable from measured quantities of the same
object; none can be bought with deterministic internal signals.
