# Patient A 500-Year Hardening Plan (Long-Form Vitals Edition)

Date: May 26, 2026  
Classification: Alias-only, privacy-preserving  
Subject: Patient A

## Founder Abstract
This document defines a 500-year hardening architecture for Patient A that treats safety, evidence, and continuity as a single system. The central objective is to keep care quality stable across tool changes, staff turnover, governance shifts, and environmental disruption.

## Vitals-First Mission
- Preserve life-critical continuity of care.
- Keep all high-impact decisions traceable to evidence.
- Prevent drift from safety doctrine across generations.
- Sustain financial and operational reliability without identity leakage.

## Core Vitals Framework
### Physiologic Vitals
- Cardiovascular stability envelope.
- Respiratory resilience envelope.
- Neurologic baseline consistency profile.
- Metabolic and renal trend integrity.
- Inflammation and immune variance tracking.

### Safety Vitals
- Contraindication detection sensitivity.
- Drug-interaction and protocol-collision detection.
- Escalation latency for red-flag events.
- False reassurance suppression index.

### System Vitals
- Evidence freshness half-life.
- Control drift index.
- Incident recurrence rate.
- Recovery time to stable baseline.
- Succession readiness score.

### Trust Vitals
- Provenance completeness rate.
- Audit replay success rate.
- Consent continuity confidence.
- Oversight quorum reliability.

## 12-Pronged Hardening Architecture
1. Identity and consent integrity.
- Alias-only records; no direct identifiers in operating artifacts.
- Multi-party consent checkpoints for irreversible actions.
- Rotating consent attestation with immutable decision log.

2. Evidence provenance chain.
- Every critical assertion linked to source and review date.
- Evidence quality tiers and stale-evidence timeouts.
- Automatic downgrade of confidence when provenance weakens.

3. Clinical risk gates.
- Fail-closed rule under uncertain contraindications.
- Mandatory second-pass review on red-zone vitals.
- Decision freeze when conflicting evidence exceeds threshold.

4. Data minimization boundaries.
- Least-privilege access by role and context.
- Split storage domains for sensitive and operational data.
- Periodic purge/retention compliance checks.

5. Model and tool trust boundaries.
- Capability allowlists for each agent/tool role.
- Human sign-off before high-impact protocol changes.
- Mismatch alarms when tool output exceeds confidence band.

6. Change control and release governance.
- Two-person approval for safety-impacting updates.
- Pre-release checklist mapped to hardening policy.
- Rollback-ready release packages for all critical updates.

7. Incident response and recovery.
- Triage tiers with explicit owner-on-call mapping.
- Evidence snapshot at incident start and closure.
- Post-incident correction loop with recurrence controls.

8. Financial continuity controls.
- Minimum runway and reserve covenant thresholds.
- Donation/funding intake controls with reconciliation checks.
- Risk limits for single-source funding dependence.

9. Legal/regulatory adaptability.
- Jurisdictional policy overlays and periodic legal review.
- Evidence packet format ready for external audits.
- Escalation path for regulatory conflict.

10. Human oversight and succession.
- Operator competency matrix and renewal cadence.
- Shadow-to-lead transition playbooks.
- Succession simulation every five years.

11. Infrastructure resilience.
- Backup/restore verification cadence.
- Offline-safe operating fallback for critical workflows.
- Integrity checksums for long-memory archives.

12. Anti-drift long-memory doctrine.
- Canonical doctrine snapshots with signed attestations.
- Drift-detection diffs across policy and implementation.
- Governance renewal without provenance loss.

## Epoch Roadmap
### Epoch 0: Years 0-2 (Stabilization)
Objectives:
- Establish baseline for all physiologic, safety, system, and trust vitals.
- Implement minimum viable controls for each prong.
- Validate fail-closed gate behavior with simulation drills.

Entry criteria:
- Alias policy active.
- Baseline vitals schema approved.
- Incident ownership roster assigned.

Exit criteria:
- 95% provenance completeness for high-impact decisions.
- Red-zone escalation under target latency.
- Quarterly gate pass rate above threshold.

### Epoch 1: Years 3-10 (Institutional Hardening)

- Add independent review and conflict-of-interest checks.
- Build annual doctrine stress test discipline.

Entry criteria:
- Epoch 0 closure attestations complete.
- Multi-role review workflow active.

Exit criteria:
- Sustained low drift index.
- Repeatable audit replay success.
- Incident recurrence below set ceiling.

### Epoch 2: Years 10-50 (Intergenerational Continuity)
Objectives:
- Preserve policy meaning across staff and tool transitions.
- Formalize compatibility and migration standards.
- Keep evidence chains intact through platform shifts.

Entry criteria:
- Succession program operational.
- Long-memory archive standard in production.

Exit criteria:
- Cross-generation handoff pass rate stable.
- Historical evidence replay integrity maintained.

### Epoch 3: Years 50-500 (Civilizational Durability)
Objectives:
- Maintain low-assumption doctrine readability.
- Re-validate axioms against emerging risks.
- Sustain governance with minimal identity exposure.

Entry criteria:
- Doctrine snapshots and checksum lineage complete.
- Legal and ethical review cadence institutionalized.

Exit criteria:
- Continuous continuity with no unresolved critical drift events.

## Gate and Scoring Model
Each gate includes:
- Gate ID
- Objective
- Required evidence bundle
- KPI thresholds
- Fail conditions
- Escalation owner
- Recovery protocol reference

Scoring bands:
- Green: Safe to proceed.
- Yellow: Proceed with mitigation and review.
- Red: Stop; fail closed until corrected.
